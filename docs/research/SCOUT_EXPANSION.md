# Expanded billiards source scout

Research checked 2026-11-04. This expands the first physics guide into experimental metrology, coaching language, real-world player control, computation, and audiovisual feedback. It adds sources and exposes where evidence runs out. **It contains no game-code changes.**

## What changed in this scout

The first guide answered idealized mechanics well, but leaned on a small number of authors and did not show enough evidence from independent experiments, full-table calibration, cue biomechanics, or sensory feedback. This scout deliberately looks across those boundaries. It sorts claims by what the source actually did: a derivation is not an experiment, a table-specific fit is not a universal coefficient, a robot-planning paper is not a teaching standard, and a visually persuasive simulator is not a reference measurement.

The strongest new practical source is a robot billiards project that fitted friction on a real pool table and reported that its apparatus could **not** identify ball–ball friction and restitution cleanly. Other valuable additions study cue-tip impact experimentally, film professional strokes at 5,000 frames per second, couple collision physics with sound and visual cues, and actively choose tests that reduce uncertainty about real table dynamics.

## 1. Real-table experiments and measurements

| Primary source | What the work actually measured | Useful for our research | Boundary to preserve |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Nierhoff, Heunisch & Hirche, *Strategic play for a pool-playing robot* (ARSO 2012)](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1155344/988748.pdf) | Tracked rolling balls on a full-size pool table using a ceiling camera. Measured sliding friction by pulling a loaded three-ball rack over the cloth at controlled speeds from 0.01 to 1 m/s with a force/torque sensor. Fit sliding and rolling terms and measured 1,217 cushion contacts across speed and incidence angle. | Independent example of a physics model fitted to one actual pool table, plus a reproducible apparatus design and measured cushion response. A simulation can therefore look convincingly calibrated while an important parameter remains unidentified. Its coefficients use its own model conventions and should not be copied into Long Rail without mapping equations and units. |

It addresses its studied experimental setup; do not convert “twofold accuracy” into a blanket promise for every speed, cloth, cue sport or cluster. |

Read the Japanese full source before using its numeric results. |

### Two source corrections that affect calibration

**Ball types matter.** A 2023 collision study calls its test objects “regulation billiard balls,” but describes them as **61 mm and 205 g**. The WPA pool specification is 57.15 mm and 156–170 g. Those experiment balls are much closer to a different cue-sport size class; its findings may check collision mechanisms but should not directly set pool-ball mass or collision calibration. In that same experiment, regulation balls rolled at `0.80 ± 0.05 m/s` along an inclined track onto a stationary ball on a PVC bench, and camera measurements were made overhead. See the [full article page and methods](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mechrescom.2023.104149) and [WPA ball specification](https://wpapool.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/RECOMMENDED-EQUIPMENT-SPECIFICATIONS.pdf#page=5).

**A higher-grip cue can transfer more spin while lowering speed.** The 2023 Cross study directly motivates a cue-speed × vertical-offset measurement matrix. Its abstract establishes the measured variables, but without the paper's full figures and conditions, the matrix is a study target rather than a source-provided game lookup table.

## 2. Real players: strength and control are distributions

| Primary source | What it observed | How to apply it carefully |
|---|---|---|
| [Kornfeind et al., *Movement variability of professional pool billiards players on selected tasks* (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.proeng.2015.07.240) | Twenty elite players performed 18 defined follow, draw, stop and break tasks. The study used eight motion cameras at 250 Hz, a 5,000 Hz camera and a 5 kHz cue accelerometer. Cue velocity at impact varied less than many other inputs; cue elevation and offset showed much larger shot-to-shot/player variation. Mean cue motion was nearly unaccelerated at contact for ordinary tasks, while breaks were an exception. | Separate repeatable game-level shot classes from natural stroke variation; use ranges for cue speed, tip offset and elevation, rather than pretending one user control point equals one canonical shot. | Twenty elite players and selected tasks are not the full population. The article reports cue kinematics, not a universal cue-ball speed associated with “soft,” “medium” or “power.” Contact-time accuracy is limited by 5 kHz sampling. |
| [Haar, van Assel & Faisal, *Motor learning in real-world pool billiards* (Scientific Reports, 2020)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7674448/) | Thirty beginners with almost no pool experience performed 300 repetitions of a constrained shot on a five-foot table with 48 mm balls. Seventeen body-motion sensors and a high-speed camera measured body movement and ball trajectories. Performance showed both quick and slow learning components; outcome and movement variability changed over repeated trials. | Demonstrates that players learn an instrumented control task differently and that repeatability is user-dependent. A cue-input setting should have transparent physical meaning and produce stable feedback. | Novices, one repeated shot, a small table and smaller balls; this studies motor learning, not tournament ball dynamics or expert definitions of stroke power. |
| [Chung et al., *Development of new 9-ball test protocols for assessing expertise in cue sports* (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7852083/) | Developed skill tests that distinguish performance across playing levels, including repeatable pocketing and cue-ball-control tasks. | Helps create user tests for aim, speed control, positioning and consistency instead of evaluating a game only by whether it runs. | Expertise assessments do not define universal physical speed categories or replace shot-physics validation. |

This evidence makes one useful distinction: “hard” as a **human cueing movement** and “hard” as a **simulated cue-ball launch speed** are not identical. If we expose a mouse or slider as the input, we should either map it to a specified launch state or let players select a stroke and show the resulting ball state. Casual categories describe a useful experience, not a unit of force.

## 3. Formal standards and the absence of universal shot labels

| Scout result | Finding |
|---|---|
| [WPA Recommended Equipment Specifications](https://wpapool.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/RECOMMENDED-EQUIPMENT-SPECIFICATIONS.pdf) | Specifies equipment dimensions, cushion height and pocket geometry. Section 8 also gives a qualitative table-speed/cushion test: a center-ball, level-cue, firm stroke should send a ball through the foot spot at least four to four-and-a-half table lengths without jumping. This offers a repeatable **whole-table cushion test**, not a measured cue force or a universal speed named “firm.” |
| [Alciatore's current typical-speed page](https://drdavepoolinfo.com/faq/speed/typical/) | Gives practical soft/slow/medium/fast/power/break ball-speed teaching bands. It is authored instruction, not an official rule or a distribution of measured player strokes. |
| [Pool table Stimpmeter proposal](https://drdavepoolinfo.com/instructional-articles/pb-review/stimpmeter/) | Proposes a physical ramp and test conditions to compare cloth/table speeds; notes room/table/ball conditions and humidity/temperature can matter. The author says this is not a universally adopted pool testing standard. |
| Informal definitions | I found no governing-body threshold that standardizes short/medium/long shot distance or exact mph ranges for touch/firm/hard strokes. Retain project bins as labeled proposals and define the measured quantity beside them. |

WPA’s four-to-four-and-a-half-length benchmark translates to roughly **10.16–11.43 meters of total path** if one “table length” is treated as the 2.54 m 9-foot playing length. That path includes cushion rebounds and is a rail-system benchmark. It does not mean a ball's uninterrupted sliding distance or one stroke on an untouched lane. The robust game target is to measure its exact protocol—start, route, stroke description and rebounds—before using it as a rail benchmark.

| [Leckie & Greenspan, *Pool Physics Simulation by Event Prediction 1: Motion Transitions* (2005)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/ICG-2005-28403) | Event-based solver predicts exact event time and position for model equations and state transitions such as sliding-to-rolling. Good reference for efficient replay and event timing. | Closed-form event prediction means the solver is internally exact under its assumptions. | [Sargaana et al., *Collaborative billiARds* (IFIP 2005)](https://dl.ifip.org/db/conf/iwec/icec2005/SargaanaFLRW05.pdf) | Early AR pool system ties audio/visual events to collisions and tested interaction features with ten users. | [Kiefl, *Pooltool: A Python package for realistic billiards simulation* (JOSS, 2024)](https://github.com/ekiefl/pooltool/blob/main/paper/paper.md) | A maintained, peer-reviewed simulation platform focused on trajectories, events and analysis. Its author paper lays out features, design goals and limitations of adjacent open-source simulators; code and tests are available. | A useful independent implementation/reference, not ground truth. Its parameters and analytic models need evidence mapping to the physical table we want to reproduce. |
| [Zhang et al., *Closing the Dynamics Gap through Self-Directed Robotic Interaction* (submitted 2 November 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02691) | New robotics preprint actively selects real billiard shots that reveal where its simulation predicts the real table least accurately. In simulation, with hidden parameters over 25 conditions and at most 30 shots, it reduced held-out ball-position error relative to an unfitted/baseline model. It also demonstrates a small real-pool-table deployment. | The 72% reduction is a **sim-to-sim hidden-physics** result; the paper's real-table case is a proof of concept, not a calibrated WPA accuracy guarantee. It is a recent preprint accepted as a late-breaking CoRL result. |
| [Nierhoff, Heunisch & Hirche, *Strategic play for a pool-playing robot* (TUM, 2012)](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1155344/988748.pdf) | Fits a friction model using a real pool table, then uses it for stroke outcome prediction. It records where values came from and which parameters could not be determined. | The accessible paper does not identify table dimensions. It simplifies cue collision to center hits for planning and acknowledges unmeasured parameters; use it for experimental method and honest uncertainty tracking. |

### An audiovisual research result with unusually useful caveats

The [Collaborative billiARds paper](https://dl.ifip.org/db/conf/iwec/icec2005/SargaanaFLRW05.pdf) is not a physics-accuracy study, but it connected impact events with four different audio cues and visual responses. Ten occasional/non-players rated a feature-complete setup higher than configurations that removed a feature. Its own limitations are instructive: small informal study, center-only hits, no ball angular motion, and the authors explicitly proposed more realistic audiovisual cues as future work. It supports testing event feedback and accessibility; it does **not** validate a linear “force equals loudness and pitch” model.

General physically based sound work [models contact force, impact location, resonant material and impact speed](https://avanzini.di.unimi.it/downloads/publications/avanzini_icme02.pdf), but our search did not find a strong open, controlled perceptual dataset of recorded pool cue/ball, ball/ball, ball/cushion and pocket sounds, all paired with measured collision impulse and table material. That is a concrete source gap. A later audio scout should target first-party recordings or design a listening and measurement protocol rather than derive sound from coefficient guesses.

## 5. New questions this source diversity exposed

1. **Can the model parameters actually be identified independently?** One end position can be matched by changing launch speed, sliding friction, rolling resistance or spin. Measure trajectory speed and spin through the transition; the Nierhoff robot paper demonstrates that some contact parameters remain unidentifiable even with a full table and sensors.
2. **Is every pooled definition tied to a type of table and ball?** The 2023 collision study's 61 mm, 205 g balls differ substantially from WPA pool equipment. Record table size, cloth, ball material/size/mass, table condition and cue setup for each parameter set.
3. **How do streaks or painted markings translate the hidden rotation into perception?** A user needs to see spin state persist through rolling, impact and rail bounce. A stripe animation can expose physical state; its rate should follow simulated angular velocity, not the UI's selected offset.
4. **What sound dimensions signal the collision?** Candidate physical inputs include normal impulse or contact speed, cushion/pocket material, ball spin and room response. The best mapping remains an empirical audio/perception question; collision event timing is a physical fact, while mix/volume/texture are designed feedback.
5. **How much input calibration should be skill-dependent?** Professional strokes vary in technique while achieving similar outcomes. A game may expose an intuitive power gesture but must define its output. An optional assist can make impact state explicit without changing the physics preset.
6. **Which tests expose a defect fastest?** Use a distinct experiment per parameter: clean rolling travel for rolling resistance; marked spin and travel for sliding-to-roll; cue speed + offset for strike transfer; rolling/full and stun/cut collision for throw; calibrated angle/speed/spin grid for cushions; measured mouth, shelf and approach speed for pockets. Do not fit every unknown against one break shot or final rack state.
7. **What is the uncertainty on our source values?** A source that publishes one number without confidence intervals, raw data or full shot conditions should set a tuning starting point, not a pass/fail tolerance. Use held-out measurements and show uncertainty.

## 6. Best next reading sequence

For a useful 60–90 minute source tour, read the professional cue-motion abstract, then the full collision-method paper for the friction/rack experiment, the experimental French billiard tribology paper for the contact-state sequence, the Collaborative billiARds paper for audio/visual feedback design and its weaknesses, and finally the 2026 CUE preprint for active calibration. Use the prior [physics guide](REAL_WORLD_POOL_PHYSICS.md) to translate each paper's table, ball and units into the game.

For calibration work later, the highest-value new source may be Nierhoff et al. rather than the biggest equations in a paper: it shows which table properties can be measured with available sensors and where a seemingly sophisticated pool simulator still lacks enough evidence.

## Scout trace and limits

This scout used four isolated source lanes and a fresh mathematical/source review. Detailed appendices: [experimental work](scout_experimental_sources.md), [instruction and measurement](scout_instruction_sources.md), [simulation and robotics](scout_simulation_sources.md), and [sensory feedback and control](scout_sensory_control_sources.md). The review in [physics-research-review.md](physics-research-review.md) found no blocking numerical error in the earlier guide. The source appendices retain their own provenance and disagreements rather than averaging models into a universal parameter set.

We did not benchmark closed commercial games, gather original sound recordings, or measure a physical table. The new robotics study is a recent preprint; the cue-impact journal page does not expose its full paper; and several instructional and video sources inform practice rather than calibrated physics. No game implementation was performed.
